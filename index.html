READ THE MODEL, NOT A PROMISE

What this experiment tells you

Timing is the experiment

The endpoint returns 503 to attempts started before recovery, then 200. Fixed latency applies to both. The outage does not react to request volume.

Jitter is a tradeoff

Full jitter samples an integer delay from zero to the backoff bound. It can spread retries, but it may use the attempt budget before recovery. One seed is one realization.

Bring this to a review

Use the exported evidence to discuss policy choices. There is no capacity model, real network, SDK configuration, or proof that a request is safe to repeat.

Timing rules and metric definitions

After failed attempt k, the bound is min(cap, base × 2^(k−1)). The delay is the larger of that policy’s delay and the synthetic Retry-After floor, measured from response completion. The deadline is relative to each client’s launch. Attempts cannot start at or after it; successful responses exactly at the deadline count. A response crossing it is cancelled.

Peak retries counts starts in fixed 250 ms buckets aligned to t=0, excluding all first attempts. Amplification is total attempts divided by clients. P95 uses the nearest rank among successful clients only; failures are reported separately. The visual chart may combine buckets and always labels its width.

Background: AWS on backoff and jitter and MDN on Retry-After. The floor here is a synthetic duration, not an HTTP header parser.
